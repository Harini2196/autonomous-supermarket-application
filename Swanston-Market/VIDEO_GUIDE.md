## 0:00–0:35 — Introduction

Introduce yourself, show your face and student ID, and introduce Swanston Market: a fictional unattended 48-product supermarket. Explain the separation between the shopping world and the rule-based Store Brain. Say that the model tests internal consistency rather than predicting a real store.

## 0:35–1:20 — Model & setup

Open Model & setup. Show A$3,488.80 initial stock, the A$40,000 cap and A$15,000 operating cash. Explain why unused inventory budget is not added to cash. Show a perishable product, its cost/price and shelf life, and an ambient household item. Describe lunch, top-up and essentials missions. Point out that stock is derived from movements, not freely editable.

## 2:15–3:10 — Inventory, decision and daily evidence

Open Inventory & brain. Explain low stock, sold-out conditions, expiry and autonomous replenishment. Explicitly note zero cash-constrained and zero slow-mover events in this run. Choose one displayed case, read its product, day, evidence sequence and order quantity. Click its evidence link, select all record types and show the order. Change to the next day and show the matching receipt with the same order reference. Explain payment at order time, fixed one-day lead time, and no Day 60 ordering.

## 3:10–4:05 — Trace a report figure

In Daily audit, select Day 1 and all products. Show the day's revenue and transactions. Expand a transaction and note its transaction ID and total. Select sale records and find matching line(s): quantity × unit selling price produces the total. Raw record money is cents. Explain that daily totals sum transactions and the 60-day report sums retained daily totals; Assurance checks this chain. Show the day selector covering 1–60 and the closing product inventory table.

## 4:05–5:15 — Three findings and next steps

Open Commercial findings. Briefly explain all three complete chains:

1. Ready meals contribute the most gross profit; mission preferences and assumed margins drive it. Validate real demand and add the excluded cost model before any lease commitment.
2. Perishable waste reflects shelf-life exposure and synchronized opening lots. Trial a smaller opening allocation and staggered receipts while monitoring lost demand.
3. Unmet demand exposes forecast/lead-time limitations. Test carefully targeted safety stock, balancing service, cash and waste. Lost units are not guaranteed recoverable sales.

Use the actual figures shown and mention each material limitation instead of claiming optimisation or real-world accuracy.

## 5:15–6:00 — Assurance, AI and close

Open Assurance. Show PASS, 60/60 days and the retained history counts. Explain opening + receipts − sales − expiry = closing stock and opening cash + revenue − replenishment = closing cash. Show the full evidence download link and state that completed history cannot be edited through the app. Mention a test that intentionally corrupts evidence and is detected.

Explain Codex's assistance and your own actual review/change decisions; do not read the development examples as if you personally performed them. Close with the need for real operating data and cost calibration. Check duration (4–8 minutes), audio, visible face/ID, MP4 format and playback before uploading the video and source ZIP to the LMS.
